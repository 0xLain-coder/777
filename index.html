FOM-1 rules · Learn

  • Pen and blank paper are always on the desk. The book is closed more often than open.
  • Predict before you read. Cover the next line of a derivation and guess it.
  • Try to prove before you read the proof. Give any theorem five honest minutes first.
  • No highlighting. Rewrite each idea: statement → why true → one example → one non-example.
  • One idea per page.
  • End every block with a “Still fuzzy:” line.

FOM-2 rules · Practice

  • Pick the set before the timer starts. Choosing is part of the 5-min set-up.
  • Use the tier ladder — practise at the right difficulty, not the comfortable one.
  • Use the stuck ladder. Struggle on purpose — but with a leash.
  • Mark every problem C / H / X the moment you finish it.
  • Never skip logging. An unlogged wrong problem is one you'll get wrong again.
  • No phone, even for “just checking a formula”. Use your notes.

FOM-3 rules · Prove & Lock

  • Write for an examiner. Every claim has a reason. Run the 10-point checklist.
  • Error-log every H and X from today.
  • Update the retrieval queue: add today's results, process due cards.
  • Finish with the Milestone check. Tick or untick honestly — no partial credit, no “nearly”.

Subject notes

Maths · Mon & Thu

  • Treat coaching maths as Tier 1–2 reinforcement for FOM, not a substitute for it.
  • Answer-hunting quietly erodes proof habits. Each Maths evening, pick one problem and write it up as a full solution.
  • Any problem where you can't justify a step in writing → FOM error log.

Physics · Tue & Fri

  • Derive, don't memorise. Re-derive the formula of the day once.
  • Units and limiting cases in every numerical: do the units work? What if a quantity → 0 or → ∞?
  • Diagram first. If you can't draw it, you don't understand it yet.
  • Split roughly 70% problems / 30% theory.

Chemistry · Wed & Sat

  • Wednesday: physical-chemistry numericals + organic mechanisms (draw every arrow, don't just look).
  • Saturday: inorganic recall cards (spaced) + organic reaction chains.
  • Inorganic is a memory subject — cards, 10 minutes at a time, never marathon sessions.

Sunday catch-up rules

  • List the backlog by subject (3 min, inside your first slot) — then work the lagging subject first, not the favourite.
  • PCM (13:15–14:15): 20 min each. If one subject is far behind, use 40 / 10 / 10 — but keep all three alive.
  • CS + English (14:30–15:30): 35 + 25. CS = one proper program or chapter; English = one essay + vocabulary review.
  • Don't turn catch-up into a second FOM day. Large PCM backlog → tell the coaching teacher. That's what the Doubt Log is for.

The 45-minute rule

Total homework inside the 12:00–18:00 coaching block, as three 15-minute slots. Everything else in the breaks is rest.

SlotWhenUse it for
AFirst break of the dayRoutine homework from the morning lecture
BSnack break — eat and stand up for 5 minutes firstEasy problems, formula cards, or doubt-list clean-up
CLast break before the final lecture3-line recall of the day's hardest topic + Doubt Log update
Everything else in the breaks is rest: eat, drink, move, talk to a human being.

Execution vs understanding

  • Downtime = execution. Routine homework, formula sheets, doubt lists, flashcards.
  • Home (E1) = understanding. Conceptual work, hard problems, derivations.
  • If you can't do a problem in 10 minutes, don't grind in a break. Doubt Log → E1 or the teacher.

What NOT to do in downtime

  • No FOM. Deep work needs unbroken focus; 15-minute fragments are wasteful.
  • No phone scrolling. It makes breaks feel longer and more tiring.
  • Don't skip every break to “save time” — chronic break-skipping is the fastest road to a 4 pm crash.
  • Don't do homework while eating. You'll do both badly.

The 10-point proof checklist

Before you call anything a proof.

0 / 10 passed

Good vs bad milestones

✓ Ten examples of the right shape
  • Reproduce the √2 irrationality proof unaided from memory.
  • Prove the section's main theorem without looking — every step justified, in ≤ 15 lines.
  • Solve 8 of 10 Tier 1 problems with zero hints in 40 minutes.
  • Solve 2 Tier 2 problems fully, each in under 20 minutes.
  • Explain the key idea of the section aloud for 3 minutes without notes.
  • Find the flaw in a given faulty proof, and repair it.
  • Write a model solution to one Tier 3 problem that a classmate could follow.
  • Re-derive formula F from first principles with no reference.
  • Classify 10 mixed problems by the technique needed — in 10 minutes, without solving them.
  • Make 3 variations of a solved problem, then solve all three.
✗ Bad milestones — rewrite them
  • “Study chapter 3”
  • “Do 20 problems”
  • “Understand induction”

None of these can be ticked or unticked by an outsider. A good milestone is checkable, observable and binary.

The Milestone Rule in practice

The early-stop rule. If you hit the Milestone with 30 minutes or more left in the FOM window, stop FOM for the day. Do not start extra problems. Convert the time into rest — a walk, an early lunch, a stretch, or a longer Nap 1.
The integrity guard. The Milestone must have been written the night before, with the evidence saved in the day's folder. A milestone invented after you feel tired is not a milestone — it's an excuse with a checkbox.

Nap 1 · pre-coaching

20 MIN
  • Goal: arrive at coaching alert for the 6-hour block.
  • Lunch ends at 11:20. Keep lunch moderate — no food coma.
  • Set the alarm before lying down — 20 minutes, no snooze.
  • Lie flat in a dim room. Phone face-down, out of reach.
  • If sleep won't come: eyes closed, breathe in 4 / out 6 for the full 20 min. That still lowers sleepiness.
  • After: water, face wash, two minutes of standing stretches.
  • Running late? Shrink to 10 minutes. Never skip it entirely.

Nap 2 · post-coaching

60 MIN · FIXED
  • Goal: recover from six hours of lectures so E1 is productive, not a fight.
  • Home → water → shoes off → straight to bed. Nothing heavier than a fruit before the nap.
  • Dark room, phone outside, alarm for 60 min (19:20).
  • Waking groggy for 10–20 min is normal — that's why the 15-min reset exists: water, face wash, bright light, a real snack. No study until 19:35.
  • Can't fall asleep at night on 3 nights running → trim to 45 min.
  • Never extend past 60 min. Extra nap time borrows from tonight's sleep.
  • No snooze. The alarm is the end of the nap.
  • Coaching ran late? Nap 2 still happens at full length — it just starts later, and E2 is dropped first.

Nap 3 · micro-nap

15 MIN
  • Goal: a quick reboot before the final study block (E2).
  • Alarm for 12 minutes — the extra three are for settling down.
  • Lie down or recline in a chair. Dim light.
  • If you can't sleep, 10 minutes of slow breathing with eyes closed counts.
  • Then the 10-minute reset: water, light, lay out the CS and English material.
  • Saturday: no Nap 3. It exists to reboot a final study session — and Saturday evening has none.

🚶 The Sacred Walk

STRICT · 21:55
  • Strict 15 minutes after dinner. Every night. No exceptions.
  • It physically separates study mode from sleep mode and helps digestion.
  • Phone at home or on silent. No headphones with study content.
  • Don't problem-solve while walking. Notice the street, the air, the sky.
  • Pace: easy to moderate. Not a workout.
  • Bad weather: corridor, terrace or indoor loops for the full 15 minutes. Never skipped.
  • Late dinner (family event): the walk happens after dinner whenever dinner ends.
  • Never study after the walk. Break this rule once and the boundary stops working.

😴 Sleep rules

  • Same times, every day. Wake-up ±30 min of 05:45; lights-out ±30 min of 22:45. Weekend drift is the usual cause of Monday-morning misery.
  • Room: dark, cool, quiet. A cheap eye-mask and earplugs beat willpower.
  • If you wake in the night: don't check the time or the phone. Slow breathing. Still awake after ~20 min? Sit up in dim light and read a few pages until sleepy.
  • Never study in bed. The bed is for sleep and the paper book only.
  • Sleep debt repayment: Sunday night (22:00 → 05:45, 7 h 45 m) and Saturday night (to 06:15, 7 h 30 m).

Day Modes & Safety Valves
A schedule that only works on perfect days is not a schedule. These modes keep you moving — and keep you recovering — on the other days. Switching modes is a skill, not a failure.
🟢 GREEN

Full plan

The complete timeline. Self-study 5 h 20 m on a coaching day.

🟡 YELLOW

Reduced load

Slept badly, feel flat, week has been heavy. Recovery blocks stay exactly as they are. Self-study 3 h. Lights-out 30 min earlier.

🔴 RED

Minimum viable day

Protect recovery and keep a thread of FOM alive — nothing more. Self-study ≤ 60 min. A Red day is a recovery day, not a lost day.

🟡 Yellow day — coaching day

Yellow-day self-study: 3 h (FOM 2 h, E1 45 min, E2 15 min). Sleep 7 h 30 m — earlier on purpose.

Yellow-day rules

  • Yellow is not failure. It is the schedule doing its job.
  • Don't “make up” Yellow-day work later. Drop it or move it to Sunday catch-up.
  • Three Yellow days in a row → start a deload week instead of waiting for Week 4.
  • Five or more Yellow days in a week → treat the week as Red.
  • Recovery menu (replaces FOM-3): pick two — sunlight walk (20), music or a hobby (30), stretching or yoga (15), a chat with family, tidy the desk.

🔴 Red day — the minimum viable day

At 05:45 do the check; if Red, go back to bed until 06:30. Self-study: 60 min at most.

Red-day rules

  • A Red day is a recovery day, not a lost day. Tomorrow starts clean.
  • Two Red days in one week → read the burnout section today and talk to someone.
  • If the cause is a specific worry (a test, a fight, a result), say it out loud to a parent, friend or mentor. Worries kept in your head grow.
  • The 30-minute “gentle FOM touch” is optional — it's the streak keeper, nothing more.

🤒 Sick-day protocol

Fever, vomiting, or you genuinely feel unwell.

  • Skip coaching and FOM. Tell a parent or guardian; see a doctor if it is more than a mild cold.
  • Water, light food, sleep. The naps become real rest.
  • Ask a classmate for the day's notes.
  • Return gradually: the first day back is a Yellow day, the second is Green if you feel fine.
  • A day or two of illness costs far less than a week of dragging yourself through blocks.
Never come back through a “catch-up” day. That is how one illness becomes a lost month.

🎉 Festival / family-event day

  • Keep the four anchors as far as you can: wake-up, Nap 2, the Sacred Walk, lights-out.
  • Run the Yellow-day study load — FOM-1 + FOM-2 only.
  • Don't double up the day before or after.
  • Being present at the event is part of a sustainable life.

🏖 Holiday weeks (coaching paused)

Resist the urge to turn all the freed time into study.

  • Add FOM-4: a 75-minute second Practice block after lunch and Nap 1.
  • Add 60 minutes of PCM self-study and 30 minutes of CS and English.
  • The remaining ≈ 4 hours are free: sport, friends, family, sleep.
  • Naps, the Sacred Walk, reading and lights-out stay exactly as they are.
  • Add more only after three straight Green weeks on the Energy Dashboard.

🛡 Rules that protect the schedule

  • Never double up. Missed work goes to Sunday catch-up, or is dropped. Doubling up is how one bad day becomes a bad week.
  • The FOM floor: on any day you are not ill, FOM gets at least 20 minutes — even one Tier 1 problem on a Red day. It keeps the thread alive.
  • Phone: in another room during FOM-1, FOM-2, FOM-3 and E1. Nothing for the first 30 min after waking. Never in bed. Use a physical alarm clock.
  • Desk-reset ritual (2 min) before every study block: clear the desk → water bottle → blank paper + two pens → timer visible → notes closed → phone away.
  • Tell your family the sacred times: Nap 2, the Sacred Walk, lights-out. A “Do not disturb — back at :” card on the door works.
  • One accountability buddy. Ten minutes once a week: “What did I commit to? What did I do?”
  • The comparison rule: your yardstick is your Milestones, not anyone else's hours. Hours measure sitting; Milestones measure learning.
  • The change rule: one dial per week, two weeks to judge it. No changes during exam fortnights.

The response ladder

LevelTriggerWhat to do
1Yellow for 3 daysYellow-day protocol. Turn the heaviest evening into rest. Lights-out 30 min earlier.
2Yellow for a week, or one Red dayStart a deload week now. Drop E2 and FOM-3. Extra outing. Talk to one person about how you're feeling.
3Two Red days in two weeks, 8+ ticks, or low mood/anxiety that lastsTalk to a parent or guardian and a teacher or mentor you trust. Consider seeing a doctor or counsellor. Do not wait until after the exam.
4You feel hopeless, trapped, or think about hurting yourselfThis is not a scheduling problem. Tell a trusted adult right now. In India: Tele-MANAS — free, 24×7, many Indian languages: 14416 or 1-800-891-4416. Immediate danger → local emergency number.

Things that feel like discipline but are really burnout

  • Studying through a fever or a migraine.
  • Cutting sleep “just this week”.
  • Skipping every break in the coaching block to finish homework.
  • Feeling guilty about the Free Zone or the free Saturday evening.
  • Comparing your hours with someone who posts their hours online.
  • Treating a Yellow day as a personal failure.

Things that actually protect you

  • Deload weeks scheduled in advance.
  • The Sacred Walk and the reading wind-down, every night.
  • One free half-day a week that nobody gets to cancel.
  • Telling someone early.
  • Sleep that is boring and consistent.

Board season · Class 12

  • ~6 weeks before the boards: convert FOM-2 on Mon / Wed / Fri into board prep for PCM, CS and English. Keep FOM-1 daily and the Saturday paper. Recovery blocks unchanged.
  • During the boards: exam-fortnight mode.
  • After the boards: restore the full revision schedule at once. The weeks between the boards and the ISI exam are your most valuable block — protect them.
  • Check each board's and each institute's current eligibility rules on their official sites well before applications open.

Taper · the last 4 weeks

  • No new topics. Weekday FOM = FOM-1 + FOM-2 (3 h); FOM-3 becomes free time.
  • One full paper a week at the actual exam time of day, so your body clock knows when to peak.
  • Last 3 days: retrieval queue and checklists only. Walk, sleep, eat well.
  • Eve of the exam: early dinner, bag packed (admit card, ID, pens, water), lights-out on time. No new problems.
  • Exam morning: wake at the usual time, light breakfast, 10-min mobility, a 10-minute glance at your formula sheet — nothing more.
The long view. Your goal is mathematical maturity — reaching the point where a problem you've never seen stops being frightening and starts being interesting. That is built by weeks of sleep-protected, milestone-gated work, not by a few heroic days.
